B. Who We Are
LANI Foundation is the social impact and philanthropic arm of LANI Group. We are a philanthropic, non-governmental, and social impact organisation committed to improving the wellbeing, dignity, protection, and opportunities of vulnerable and underserved populations.
We work with women, children, orphaned and vulnerable children, persons with disabilities, refugees, asylum seekers, returned migrants, widows, youth, caregivers, grassroots educators, community-based organisations, and underserved households. Our work moves beyond occasional charity by creating structured, evidence-informed interventions that respond to real needs and strengthen the systems that serve people.
Through education, livelihood support, advocacy, capacity building, child protection, reintegration support, community wellbeing, and institutional strengthening, we help people and organisations build resilience, agency, and pathways toward sustainable growth.

About LANI Foundation
We are building systems that work for people through inclusive development, advocacy, capacity building, and stronger institutions.
Who We Are
LANI Foundation is the social impact and philanthropic arm of LANI Group. We work to improve the wellbeing, dignity, protection, and opportunities of vulnerable and underserved populations through practical programmes, strategic partnerships, and institutional strengthening.
Our work focuses on people and communities affected by poverty, exclusion, displacement, disability, unsafe migration, gender inequality, weak support systems, and limited access to opportunity. We believe that social impact must be structured, compassionate, accountable, and sustainable.
Our Story
LANI Foundation was established to convert compassion into organised development action. We recognise that vulnerable people often face challenges that cannot be solved by one-time support alone. For this reason, our work combines direct service delivery, advocacy, capacity development, mentorship, institutional strengthening, and community collaboration.
Our story is rooted in the belief that people deserve dignity, opportunity, and systems that respond to their needs. As part of LANI Group, we draw on wider institutional knowledge, professional networks, and development experience to strengthen the way we support communities and partners.
Vision and Mission
Our Vision: To build a society where vulnerable people are protected, communities are empowered, and institutions are responsive enough to provide inclusive, effective, and sustainable support.
Our Mission: To empower individuals and communities through education, entrepreneurship, mentorship, advocacy, capacity building, and institutional strengthening.
Our Purpose
Our purpose is to reduce vulnerability, expand opportunity, strengthen social systems, protect dignity, support self-reliance, and create long-term community transformation. We work so that children can grow in safer environments, youth and women can access opportunity, returned migrants can rebuild with dignity, and institutions can serve people better.

1. Mapping Children’s Homes in Lagos State: Building Evidence for Better Child Welfare
Category: Child Protection & Welfare SystemsPeriod: 2012–2013Location: Lagos StateThematic Alignment: BELONG / Evidence & Insights
Story
For children living in care homes, support must begin with understanding. Before meaningful interventions can be designed, institutions and partners need to know the realities children face, the needs of caregivers, and the conditions within care environments.
Between 2012 and 2013, we conducted a comprehensive operational mapping and structural field assessment of orphanage and care homes across Lagos State. The work was designed to move child welfare support away from assumptions and toward evidence. By engaging care-home realities directly, we gathered information on infrastructure gaps, resource shortfalls, caregiver needs, and the lived conditions of institutionalised children.
This intervention reflected one of our strongest convictions: child protection must go beyond goodwill. It must be structured, informed, documented, and accountable. Through the mapping exercise, we helped create a clearer picture of the needs within care settings, making it easier to understand where support was most needed and how future interventions could be better designed.
The project also helped strengthen our approach to child welfare systems. Rather than treating vulnerable children as passive recipients of charity, the work recognised that children need safer environments, stronger caregiver systems, better documentation, and institutions capable of responding consistently to their wellbeing.
Why It Matters
This mapping work became an important foundation for our child protection and welfare systems approach. It showed that effective support begins with listening, assessing, documenting, and understanding. It also reinforced the need for care homes and social welfare actors to operate with stronger evidence, clearer needs assessment, and improved service planning.
Impact Focus
Improved understanding of care-home needs and operational realities
Strengthened evidence base for child welfare programming
Supported more informed planning for orphaned and vulnerable children
Reinforced the importance of documentation in child protection
Contributed to LANI Foundation’s BELONG Framework for safer growth environments

2. Strengthening Caregiver Readiness Through CPR, First Aid and Emergency Preparedness
Category: Child Protection, Capacity Building & Emergency PreparednessPeriod: 2013Thematic Alignment: BELONG / Capacity Building
Story
Children in care homes depend not only on shelter and goodwill, but also on the readiness of the adults and institutions responsible for their safety. In emergency situations, the knowledge and preparedness of caregivers can make a critical difference.
In 2013, we designed and implemented professional training in cardiopulmonary resuscitation, basic first aid, and disaster preparedness for care-home staff. The intervention responded to a practical need: caregivers working with vulnerable children require more than compassion; they also need the skills, confidence, and tools to respond when emergencies occur.
The training was delivered as part of our wider commitment to strengthening child-facing institutions. It equipped care-home staff with emergency response knowledge and supported participating care settings with emergency first-aid boxes. This combination of training and practical materials reflected the Foundation’s approach to capacity building: knowledge should be useful, applicable, and connected to the realities of frontline care.
The intervention also demonstrated how small but targeted systems improvements can strengthen child welfare. By helping caregivers become more prepared, the Foundation contributed to safer care environments and reinforced the importance of institutional readiness in child protection.
Why It Matters
Emergency preparedness is a vital part of child protection. Caregivers are often the first responders in care environments, and their ability to act quickly, calmly, and correctly can influence the safety and wellbeing of children. Our work helped position caregiver capacity as a core part of welfare systems, not an optional add-on.
Impact Focus
Built practical emergency response capacity among care-home staff
Supported safer care environments for vulnerable children
Provided emergency first-aid materials to participating care settings
Strengthened the connection between child welfare and caregiver preparedness
Advanced our BELONG Framework through practical protection support

3. Orphanage Library and Literacy Initiatives: Creating Learning Spaces for Vulnerable Children
Category: Education & Literacy AdvancementPeriod: 2016–2019Locations: Home of God’s Grace Mission Orphanage and Agnus Dei OrphanageThematic Alignment: BELONG / EMPOWER / Quality Education
Story
For children in vulnerable settings, books can open doors to confidence, imagination, learning, and future opportunity. Literacy is more than the ability to read; it is a foundation for self-expression, academic development, social mobility, and personal dignity.
Between 2016 and 2019, We launched education enrichment initiatives designed to strengthen learning culture in care settings. This included a major library setup project at Home of God’s Grace Mission Orphanage, educational book donations at Agnus Dei Orphanage, and reading sessions created to encourage children’s engagement with books and learning.
The initiative reflected our belief that education must reach children who are often left behind. Children in care settings face many barriers, including limited access to learning materials, under-resourced environments, and reduced exposure to structured enrichment activities. By supporting libraries, books, and reading sessions, the Foundation helped create spaces where children could learn, imagine, and build confidence.
This work also connected two parts of our methodology. Through BELONG, the initiative supported children in care environments by improving their learning conditions. Through EMPOWER, it helped nurture confidence, curiosity, and the foundation for future opportunity.
Why It Matters
Education support in care settings is not just about donating books. It is about creating an environment where children feel seen, supported, and encouraged to grow. Literacy can strengthen confidence and help children develop the tools they need to participate more fully in society.
Impact Focus
Supported library development in a care-home setting
Donated educational books to strengthen learning access
Encouraged reading culture among vulnerable children
Supported education enrichment in underserved care environments
Advanced our commitment to quality education and literacy

4. Disability-Sensitive Media Training: Promoting Dignity Through Responsible Communication
Category: Gender Equality, Social Inclusion & AdvocacyPeriod: 2013–2014Thematic Alignment: ENHANCE / Advocacy & Media
Story
The way society speaks about vulnerable groups shapes how they are seen, treated, included, or excluded. For persons with disabilities, public narratives can either reinforce stigma or promote dignity, understanding, and inclusion.
Between 2013 and 2014, We conducted specialised training for journalists and media professionals on disability-sensitive reporting. The training focused on respectful terminology, ethical storytelling, dignity-based public communication, and the role of media in challenging harmful stereotypes.
This intervention recognised that inclusion is not only a policy issue; it is also a communication issue. When journalists and media actors understand how to report responsibly, they help shape public attitudes and reduce stigma. Our work therefore supported the development of more inclusive public communication systems.
The training also aligned with the Foundation’s broader commitment to gender equality and social inclusion. Persons with disabilities often face barriers created not only by physical environments, but also by language, representation, and public perception. By working with media professionals, We contributed to a more respectful and informed communication culture.
Why It Matters
Media professionals influence public understanding. Disability-sensitive reporting helps reduce stigma, protects dignity, and encourages society to see persons with disabilities as rights-holders, contributors, and active members of communities. This work reflects our commitment to advocacy, inclusion, and institutional capacity strengthening.
Impact Focus
Strengthened capacity of journalists and media professionals
Promoted disability-sensitive reporting and respectful terminology
Supported dignity-based public communication
Helped challenge harmful stereotypes and stigma
Advanced our ENHANCE Framework through media and advocacy

5. COVID-19 Risk Communication Campaign: Strengthening Public Trust During Crisis
Category: Public Health Communication, Capacity Building & Systems StrengtheningPeriod: 2020Thematic Alignment: ENHANCE / Public Health
Story
During a public health crisis, accurate communication can save lives. When misinformation spreads, communities may become fearful, confused, or resistant to protective action. In moments like this, journalists and community communicators play an important role in shaping public understanding.
In 2020, We responded to the COVID-19 crisis by deploying targeted risk communication training frameworks for journalists and community communicators. The campaign supported responsible public reporting, helped counter local misinformation, and contributed to reducing panic during a period of uncertainty.
The intervention reflected our systems-strengthening approach. Public health response is not only about hospitals and medical supplies; it also depends on trusted communication, responsible media engagement, and community-level understanding. By working with communicators, the Foundation supported the systems that help people receive accurate information and make informed decisions.
This work also reinforced our commitment to capacity building. The training helped media and community actors communicate more responsibly during crisis conditions, while supporting public awareness and community resilience.
Why It Matters
Risk communication is an essential part of public health and community wellbeing. When communicators are better equipped, communities are more likely to receive clear, accurate, and responsible information. Our COVID-19 communication work showed how capacity building can strengthen public trust and reduce harm during crisis.
Impact Focus
Supported responsible public health communication
Strengthened capacity of journalists and community communicators
Helped reduce misinformation and public panic
Contributed to community awareness during a public health emergency
Advanced our ENHANCE Framework through public health systems support
